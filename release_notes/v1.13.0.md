# Release Notes v1.13.0

This release lets you choose in which order the mower mows the area, the border and the exclusion borders, and can mow the exclusion borders on the way while mowing the area.

## Route Order

- New group **Order** in the mow settings of the map editor. The three parts of the route — **Area**, **Border** and **Exclusion borders** — can be put in any order with ▲/▼. Before, the order was fixed: area first, then the border, then all exclusion borders; or, with **Mow border CCW**, both borders first and the area last.
- New option **Mow exclusion borders while passing**: the laps around each exclusion are mowed during the area, from the point of the area pattern that is closest to the exclusion. The mower drives there, mows the laps and returns to the same point, instead of driving to every exclusion one after the other at the end. The **Exclusion borders** entry then disappears from the order, and the area reads **Area with exclusion borders**. In a test map with three exclusions, "Border, then Area with the exclusion borders" was the shortest route (541 m instead of 560 m).
- **Mow border CCW** is now called **Border laps counter-clockwise** and only sets the direction of the laps, no longer the order.
- Maps without a saved order keep their previous route: without **Mow border CCW** area, border, exclusion borders; with it border, exclusion borders, area. Recalculate the route after changing the order.
- The **Mow areas** switches (Area, Border, Excl. border) keep working with every order, also for exclusion borders mowed during the area.
- When **Mow areas** switches hide parts of the route, the maps show the drive between the remaining parts along the path the mower will take, around the exclusions. The modem calculates these drives for the upload anyway and now sends them to the maps; until they arrive, a dashed straight line stands in. The maps only drew a gap before, most visible with the exclusion borders mowed during the area and **Area** switched off.

## Map

- The **Mow areas** switches no longer jump back and forth when switched quickly one after the other. The modem answers every change with all mow settings; the answer to the first switch did not contain the second one yet and turned it back for a moment. MowMate now keeps a change it sent until the modem confirms it (at most 5 seconds).
- Calculating the route again shortly after a calculation no longer cuts off the connection to the browser. The modem was still sending the previous result to the browser; while it does, it holds back all other messages, so no progress arrived, and at the end it discarded the new route because the map was still being read. A new calculation now stops that transfer first.
- Switching **Mow areas** no longer makes the modem hang for 10–20 seconds on large maps. To connect the remaining parts of the route, it set up its path finder anew for every gap, and did so twice per change for an unsaved map. It now sets it up once and keeps the drives it found: after a map is loaded or calculated, it works them out for all combinations of the switches in the background, one at a time, so later switching answers almost at once. Inserting the exclusion laps into the area is faster as well.
- The **Mow areas** switches are locked with the note **Updating…** until the modem has applied a change and sent the new point count and route connections, which can take a second or two on large maps.
- After switching **Mow areas**, the **⚠ not synced — upload** button appears right away. The modem sent the reset upload state only with the next regular status update, so the map looked synced for a few seconds. The point counts show MowMate's own count until the modem's exact count arrives, instead of the old number.
- Connections inside the area keep the **Distance to exclusions**. Where the area pattern could not be connected within the mow area, for example across a narrow gap between two exclusions, the connection ran along the exclusions with only a few centimetres to spare. It now first looks for a way that keeps the distance and only goes closer when there is none.
